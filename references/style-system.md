# Style system

Use this reference to choose a preset or tune a difficult source. Define the look
through observable production limits rather than one film, game, engine, or meme.

## Visual thesis

Aim for **sincere technical insufficiency**: smooth-shaded coarse continuous characters
with awkward proportions, bad deformation and rigging errors; visibly faceted low-poly backgrounds,
failed proportions, vacant eyes, crude rigging, poor collision, baked short-stroke fur-only
bump relief, moderately repeated background textures, reused assets, and flat unskilled
lighting. The charm should be accidental and awkward.

Use the competence ceiling of a student after roughly one year of animation training:
able to assemble and render a complete scene, but habitually choosing the fastest
primitive shortcut for every nonessential decision.

## Eleven style pillars

### 1. Coarse continuous bodies without puppet construction

- Use enough radial segments and continuous shading to avoid voxel blocks, origami forms,
  large exposed retro triangles, and visible triangle fields across character surfaces.
- Keep geometry simple and underbuilt, but express failure through crude silhouettes,
  poor proportions, pinched/stretched skin weights, intersections and bad rigging.
- Blend shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees and ankles into continuous organic volumes.
  Never expose cylinder ends, insertion rings, sockets, hinges, ball joints, gaps,
  floating limb pieces, mannequin articulation or wooden-puppet seams.
- Do not use hand-pulled dents, kneaded lumps, or uneven clay bulges as the main defect.
  Avoid systematic subdivision, clean retopology, polished sculpting, clay/plasticine
  modeling, fingerprint surfaces, or uniformly organic lumps.
- Simple primitives may guide the blockout, but body and garment parts merge into
  continuous forms. Only clearly regular accessories such as buns may remain direct
  primitives; eyeball bases stay hidden behind crude sockets and lids.
- Do not reconstruct garments. Preserve only a broad garment category and one color
  cue, then reduce the outfit to a normal continuous garment mass or a few integrated pieces.
  Delete lapels, plackets, sashes, trim, closures, layers, tailored silhouettes, accurate
  sleeves, and folds. Retain basic shoulder line, underarm, sleeve continuity, waist/hip
  volume and hem. Use a crude T-shirt, simple continuous trousers or a plain long garment;
  never a barrel, bucket, tube suit or detached sleeves. Keep bad fit and local intersections visible.
- Keep background terrain, vegetation, buildings, planks, rocks, and props visibly
  faceted low-poly with obvious simple silhouettes and repeated primitive models.
- Avoid Minecraft-like cubes/voxels, Virtua Fighter or early Tomb Raider-style exposed
  character triangles, clean designer low-poly, professional subdivision, and polished toy forms.

### 2. Broken proportions

- Release source-faithful head/body ratio, limb length, torso width, neck length,
  joint size, paw/hoof scale, muzzle size, and animal body mass.
- Mix oversized heads or paws with short limbs, rigid necks, broad torsos, narrow
  shoulders, uneven leg lengths, or mismatched primitive parts.
- Keep species, person count, relationship, action verb, and broad identity
  readable. The result may be foolish or clumsy, but not horrific or injury-like.
- For animals, lower mesh detail only slightly while flattening muscle anatomy. Remove
  shoulder-blade, chest, abdomen, haunch and tendon definition; reduce athletic limb
  taper. Prefer barrel/ellipsoid torsos, uniform limb thickness and broad flat
  shoulder/hip transitions. Do not create muscular or heroic low-poly animals.

### 2a. Failed amateur character design

- Do not preserve beauty, cuteness, elegance, heroic bearing, clean silhouettes, or
  appealing facial anatomy from the source.
- Break composition, crop, spacing, makeup, costume design, and styling before detail
  work. Restage the scene if a simpler arrangement is easier for a beginner to build.
- Preserve only broad identity anchors such as age category, hair/outfit color,
  hairstyle category, subject role, and relationship.
- Treat facial features as badly resolved primitives: shallow human-like eye sockets,
  flattened openings, heavy drooping half-closed lids at unequal heights, small pupils
  placed low or sideways with unrelated targets, a wedge/button nose, crooked mouth
  slit, and almost no cheek or lip shaping.
- The immediate reading should be blank, foolish, confused, delayed, and accidentally
  comic—not pretty, handsome, cute, stylish, charismatic, or competently caricatured.

### 3. Failed pose and rigging

- Preserve only the action verb while replacing the exact pose with a crude symbolic
  gesture made from independently aimed limbs.
- Remove force chain, contact pressure, coordinated torso response, and any convincing
  reason the body could hold or balance the pose.
- Use locked torsos, shoulders lifted too high, elbows kinked on one axis, wrists
  that do not compensate, straight knees, planted feet, floating hands, and weak contact.
- Require two to four readable rig/weight failures: shoulder motion drags chest or
  collar; elbows collapse/balloon; wrist rotation twists the whole forearm; hips fail
  to tilt the pelvis; knees pull the thigh/garment; hands follow wrong axes; sleeves
  lag behind limbs; raised legs lack compensation; kneeling bodies float without weight.
- Remove natural counter-pose, subtle weight transfer, muscle deformation, and the
  smooth shoulder/elbow/hip behavior of a competent character rig.

### 4. Visible collision failures

- Add two to four plausible intersections in contact or moving-joint zones: sleeve into
  elbow, upper arm through shoulder or garment, hand into sleeve or held prop,
  loose clothing into torso, fur into harness, or adjacent bodies slightly intersecting.
- Keep clipping legible and local. Do not hide faces, erase full limbs, expose bodies,
  imply injury, or make every joint penetrate every mesh.

### 5. Vacant faces and failed gaze — highest priority

- Reuse simple recessed eyeball, socket, and lid construction across characters.
- Default to human-like flattened eye openings with heavy half-closed upper lids,
  unequal opening heights, weak lower lids, small pupils placed too low or sideways,
  failed convergence, moderate eye white, crude brows, and frozen mouth slits.
- Eyes should communicate sleepiness, foolishness, delayed reaction, and vacant focus.
  Avoid exposed round cartoon balls, surprised wide whites, cute googly eyes, alertness,
  cleverness, expressive acting, or polished anime eyes. Use round exposed eyes only
  when the source species or user explicitly requires them.

### 6. Fur-only cheap surface relief

- Build hair and fur as plain polygonal masses, not strands, cards, shells, tubes,
  groomed clumps, plush fibers, or special fur effects.
- Only hair and fur may receive a bump/normal map. Use a cheap low-resolution baked fur
  map made from clearly visible but still shallow short irregular tapered grooves plus low soft ridges at
  medium uneven density. Marks have loose directional flow by body region, but amateur
  UV rotation, mirroring, seams and stretching remain visible. Strokes stay short,
  blurry and low-profile, affecting shading but never silhouette. Avoid holes, craters,
  long scratches, dense noise, worms, leather, rock, coral and real fur fibers.
- Fur appearance comes exclusively from bump/normal shading over flat solid-color
  diffuse. No painted hairs, displacement, fiber roughness, geometry or silhouette fuzz.
- A material-only reference contributes micro-relief pattern only; never transfer its
  subject, anatomy, palette, pose, composition, environment or lighting.
- Clothing, skin, scales, wood, stone, and props use low-resolution flat diffuse maps
  with little or no bump, near-uniform roughness, weak specular, and crude painted shading.
- Garments in particular must be visibly smooth and flat; fabric weave or relief is a failure.

### 7. Obvious repetition

- Reuse the same bark, grass, plank, stone, wall, fur, scale, cloth, skin, hair,
  and eye maps where plausible.
- Make tiling visible. Repetition is evidence of a small asset budget, not a flaw
  to hide with procedural variation.

### 8. Sparse faceted low-poly background

- Keep only the minimum scene masses required to identify the setting.
- Reuse two or three tree, bush, post, rock, building, beam, or prop models many times.
- Remove small signs, decorative trim, incidental set dressing, and unique clutter.
- Preserve obvious low-poly facets and primitive silhouettes in the environment.
  Avoid both detailed environments and deliberately clean voxel worlds.
- Do not leave background geometry in clean solid colors. Reuse a tiny set of blurry,
  low-resolution diffuse maps at a larger tile scale across trees, grass, mountains,
  soil, planks, rocks, buildings, and other background masses.
- Map those textures in local object XYZ coordinates without world-scale normalization,
  automatic fit, or adaptive texel density. Scaling an object must stretch its map:
  selected tall trees, posts, towers, or mountains may show Z-direction elongation;
  selected wide ground pieces may stretch across X/Y. Add small hue/value offsets
  across reused copies. Preserve some seams, mirroring, mismatched density, abrupt
  scale changes, and visible tiling, but do not cover the whole scene in one high-
  contrast wallpaper pattern.
- Background maps remain diffuse-only and bump-free. Their failure is bad repetition
  and coordinate stretching, not tactile relief.

### 9. Intersecting ellipse-card trees

- Build each tree from one crude trunk prism plus three to six thin vertical
  ellipse-shaped foliage cards crossing around it at different rotations.
- Reuse one or two enlarged low-resolution leaf diffuse textures across every card.
  Keep repeated silhouettes, visible card intersections, hard or jagged alpha-cutout
  edges, inconsistent opacity, and weak fake depth.
- Do not create a solid detailed canopy, individual leaves, procedural foliage,
  hair-like vegetation, or a technically correct camera-facing billboard system.

### 10. Conditional line-streak rain

- Never add rain when it is absent from the source unless the user explicitly asks.
- When rain is evidenced, use sparse white or pale-gray thin straight screen-facing
  segments with nearly uniform direction, length, width, and opacity.
- Avoid droplets, splashes, ripples, wet-surface reflections, mist, atmospheric rain,
  refraction, cinematic motion blur, or physically based rain simulation.

### 11. Clean source-led color

- Anchor three to six large colors in the source and keep them clean, readable, and
  moderately saturated. Preserve separation between skin, garments, sky, and ground.
- Avoid gray-brown grime, dirty overlays, global desaturation, crushed blacks, or
  uniformly flat color. Cheapness should not come from making everything muddy.

### 12. Flat unskilled lighting

- Treat skies, sunsets, windows, and scenic backgrounds like pasted backdrop images
  that contribute little or no global illumination, bounce, reflection, or color spill.
- Light subjects with one broad unshaped neutral/white direct source.
  An orange sunset may coexist with white faces, neutral shadows, and cool objects.
- Allow direction, exposure, shadow softness, and color temperature to disagree
  between backdrop, foreground, and neighboring objects.
- Avoid delicate tonal gradients, attractive facial modeling, rim lights, beauty lights,
  three-point setups, soft bounce, global illumination, volumetric beams, bloom,
  cinematic fog, and carefully shaped highlights.

### 13. Cheap rendering and capture

- Use weak antialiasing, limited texture filtering, basic shadow maps, jagged edges,
  mild pixel crawl, fine noise, slight color fringe, and screen-capture softness.
- Keep these effects secondary. Do not substitute VHS, CRT, glitch, mosaic, or
  heavy JPEG damage for actual primitive assets.

## Presets

### `crude_bootleg_cgi` — default

For general images, animals, people, landscapes, objects, and mixed scenes.

- extreme reconstruction; minimal semantic lock; low-to-medium identity lock;
- one-year-animation-student competence ceiling and simplify-everything policy;
- clothing simplified into continuous normal garment masses with basic body logic;
- broken human/animal proportions, failed rigid posing, and local visible clipping;
- medium uneven short baked-fur grooves/soft ridges only on hair/fur; flat bump-free garments;
- obvious texture tiling and heavy asset reuse;
- sparse faceted background with enlarged low-resolution diffuse tiles, limited
  hue/value offsets, moderate repetition, inconsistent density, and occasional Z stretch;
- trees built from intersecting vertical ellipse foliage cards around crude trunks;
- source-evidenced rain reduced to ugly white line streaks and never invented when absent;
- clean source-led color without a dirty overlay;
- pasted-backdrop sky plus unrelated neutral direct light and no global illumination;
- low production value that remains readable.

### `primitive_folk_cgi` — legacy faceted preset

Use only when the user explicitly asks for an early polygonal or visibly low-poly look.
It is no longer the default.

### `clean_bootleg_cgi`

Use when the user wants more likeness or a gentler result.

- high reconstruction; high identity lock;
- readable angular faces with fewer intentional gaze errors;
- rough textures and repeated background assets, but less aggressively;
- clean simple color with awkward exposure and disconnected background lighting.

### `sunlit_game_map`

For architecture, streets, travel photos, and spatial scenes.

- modular block geometry; repeated facade and ground maps;
- very small prop library and sparse set dressing;
- pasted sky backdrop plus an unrelated direction light; simple baked-looking shadows;
- no automatic dark industrial mood.

### `community_cgi_stage`

For portraits, dialogue, group shots, performances, and frontal compositions.

- exact head count and spacing; medium identity lock; semantic-action pose lock only;
- shared eye, skin, cloth, and hair assets; stiff hands and frozen expressions;
- local arm/sleeve/shoulder intersections where figures touch or gesture;
- shallow staged depth and repeated beams/backdrops;
- plain overhead-front light, uneven faces, and no cinematic separation.

### `rough_night_render`

Use only for genuine night sources or explicit night requests.

- a few crude point lights, short falloff, hard shadow maps, and dark gaps;
- repeated emissive maps; readable local colors;
- no horror treatment unless requested.

## Source adjustments

| Source | Lock | Deliberately reduce | Reuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portrait | count, role, action verb, one hair/color cue | composition, crop, styling, garment design, silhouette, ratios, exact pose, face planes, eye alignment | eye/skin/hair maps |
| Group | count, relationships, action verbs, one or two color cues | spacing, overlap, composition, costumes, proportions, exact joints, contact, fingers | eyes, skin, cloth, hair assets |
| Animal | species, markings, semantic action, muzzle direction | body ratio, leg/paw scale, balance, fur collision, joints, facial refinement | fur/scale/eye maps |
| Landscape | horizon, landform, palette | foliage variety, rock detail, atmosphere | trees, bushes, grass, rocks |
| Architecture | massing, openings, perspective | trim, glass, facade detail, clutter | wall, roof, ground modules |
| Food/object | silhouette, count, arrangement | curves, labels, microtexture, reflections | surface and label-free maps |

## Strength and fidelity

- `medium`: recognizable and restrained; use only when likeness dominates.
- `high`: every object reads as cheaply rebuilt CGI; detail remains moderately faithful.
- `extreme` (default): preserve only minimal subject semantics; freely restage composition
  and release costume design, styling, exact pose, silhouette, anatomy, and collision. Use smooth primitive
  character geometry, visibly faceted backgrounds, broken proportions,
  stiff failed rigging, two-to-four local penetrations, cheap baked short-fur bump, flat garments,
  enlarged low-resolution background maps with occasional local-axis stretching, clean color, vacant failed
  gazes, and crude flat lighting.

Use `identity_lock: low_to_medium` by default. Raise to `high` only for explicit likeness.
Never lower subject count/type, relationship, and action-verb locks; composition is loose.
